## Purpose and audience

Sustainability Map is a map-first early planning tool for Los Angeles. Its primary users are urban planners and traffic engineers. Municipal stormwater staff, civil or landscape consultants, and property managers screening a retrofit are also plausible users. The original Home Buyers workspace was dropped when the product focus shifted to planning.

The app shortens early feasibility work by bringing aerial evidence, property context, a draft design and its test results into one place. A planner can see which inputs led to a recommendation, revise constraints and take the design into a field visit or engineering study. For the hackathon, the working pitch is the **Sustainability** main track and CoStar's **Inside and Out: AI for the Built World** sponsor track. The earlier discussion used the onsite hacker packet as judging context. The clearest demo is a real sustainability decision that visibly changes based on CoStar and Vexcel evidence.

| Workspace | Discovery question | Design question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Traffic & Emissions** | Which intersections or approaches show demand, recurring delay, or high modeled emissions where the data supports those signals? | What physical change could lower total CO₂ while preserving acceptable traffic operations and pedestrian and property access? |
| **Stormwater** | Where do design rainfall, terrain and drainage context suggest investigating runoff or collection? | Where could a rain garden, bioswale or similar vegetated treatment fit, and what could it temporarily store under a defined event? |

These are independent workflows. A planted curb extension can connect them when the same site supports both uses. In traffic, the user wants substantive redesigns such as lane-divider shifts, turn-lane changes, pedestrian islands, pinch points, roundabouts or jughandles when the tools can assess them. Signal timing can support a physical change. The live simulator currently evaluates a narrower set of lane reallocations. Broader geometry options require their own supported evaluation.

## User flow and design requirements

1. Open the planner map in **Traffic & Emissions** or **Stormwater**. Browse dated, sourced discovery layers or go directly to a known location. A planner can search CoStar, select a discovery feature, click the map, or use the map center with a keyboard. Select the actual CoStar search result when its property attributes matter to the analysis.
2. Start with the suggested site brief or ask a question in plain language. Examples include “Draft a rain garden here and tell me what could drain to it” and “Test physical changes to this intersection that could reduce CO₂ while protecting access.”
3. Watch the investigation in the optional agent activity panel. Show tools called, sources found or unavailable, the task underway, and the reason for a decision. Keep the sidebar focused on the question, actionable results and evidence that changes the decision. Lower-level detail can expand on demand.
4. Read one recommended physical design when supported. Explain the change, why it was selected, impacts, challenges, approvals or conflicts and what still needs field verification. A supported stormwater **Draft concept** and a traffic **Tested, rejected** layout are valuable results with their own labels.
5. Toggle the geographically aligned 2D footprint. Its geometry, comparison values and explanation must belong to the same site, workflow and input revision. Keep each tested traffic footprint inspectable with its rejection or tradeoff explanation even when the run has no winner.
6. Revise in chat or adjust a specific map constraint when necessary. Preserve the prior result with a stale label while replacement work is pending. Cancellation, site changes and late responses must never cause one result to repaint another site.
7. The stretch output is a registered **Existing / Proposed** 3D comparison with the same proposal and viewpoint. Use a relevant Matterport exterior scan for Existing and a simple agent-generated model of the approved 2D design for Proposed.

The early map feedback was to start closer in, move zoom controls clear of the LA label, smooth zooming, and keep map dragging within tighter imagery coverage with movement along angled or curved edges. Those are continuing map-quality expectations. Current coverage checks constrain the center against source geometry. Check viewport behavior again on desktop and narrow screens before claiming complete visual acceptance.

## Evidence and modeling rules

- **CoStar is core to site context.** Use reported property class, units, gross floor area, parking/loading totals where complete, and usable frontage or curb-cut records to identify operations and access impacts. Nearby geocoded records can be associated locally with touched Vexcel parcels. A match is evidence of proximity. Title, ownership and access rights need separate verification. The local commercial snapshot can describe reported vacancy or tenant context. It cannot establish changes in apartment vacancy, resident counts or site-generated traffic. Neighborhood population change would need separate, comparable Census geographies and periods.
- **Vexcel is core to the physical map.** `property/extract` gives mapped parcel and building outlines, `elements/detect` gives candidates such as crosswalks, driveways, parking and landscape, and `embedding/extract` returns ranked imagery areas. A semantic match is useful for finding where to look. Candidate boxes need verification before asserting exact dimensions, driveway position, crossing geometry or surface function. For intersections, query a bounded area around the junction. A point on a public roadway commonly misses the parcel records.
- **Rainfall and terrain have different jobs.** County contour interpolation estimates event depth in inches when contours support it. Subbasins and county drainage context locate a site in a broader system. USGS bare-earth elevation can support terrain paths and collection candidates. Those inputs do not establish flooded depth, roof drainage, curb or inlet elevations, a verified contributing area, or an overflow route.
- **Stormwater uses a storage-only event balance.** External runoff is event depth times the unioned contributing area times its runoff coefficient. Use treatment area times effective ponding depth for gross storage. Reserve capacity for direct rain on the treatment first. Report temporary external storage, excess external runoff and direct rain excess separately when available. Source depth and planner what-if assumptions stay distinct. Infiltration, drain-out, peak flow, flood protection and construction feasibility are separate questions.
- **Historical counts are tied to a location and period.** They are the number of reported movements during the named count window. Avoid the label “vehicles reported” or a vague “historical total” without date, interval and units. Traffic Flow or routing estimates could add current observed speed and delay context. They cannot replace intersection turning counts or directly measure tailpipe CO₂.
- **Traffic sustainability ranking is CO₂-first and balanced.** Require a reduction in total same-cohort modeled tailpipe CO₂, at least 98% of baseline completions, no more than two percentage points of extra entry failures, and delay, queue and noise within the existing regression limits. The noise limit uses sound energy. Use consistent offered demand, paired seeds, source-bound fleet rates and approved, labeled generated timing. Preserve turn restrictions, crossings, access and keep-clear constraints. Disclose parcel overlap as a potential impact and handle real building conflicts independently. Missing required checks can block recommendation.
- **Use source states honestly.** A source outage, no returned features, unassessed value and measured zero are different. No made-up traffic, drainage connections, source fields, synthetic demo measurements, clean data fallbacks or substitute scan. Show partial evidence and the next specific check when needed.
